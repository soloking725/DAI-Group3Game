Sketching & Animation Advice for Beginners

You said you've never made game animation before and normally only draw black-and-white — good news: that's a perfectly fine, even advantageous, place to start. Game sprite work leans on readability far more than on rendering, and B&W/grayscale is how most professionals block out a character before color ever enters the picture.

Yes, sketch first — don't feel behind for it. Silhouette and pose readability (can you tell what's happening at a glance, mid-swing, at actual game size?) is the entire job at this stage. Color is a separate, later pass — it can even be done by someone else, or added as a flat tint over your linework once the motion reads correctly. Don't let "I can't color" block you from starting.

The core workflow (how professional 2D animators actually work)

  1. Thumbnail the key poses first, not the in-betweens. Pick the 2-3 poses that tell the whole story of a move — for a melee swing: anticipation (wind-up), contact (the hit), and follow-through. Sketch these tiny and rough before anything else. If the silhouette doesn't read at thumbnail size, it won't read in-game either.
  2. Draw in silhouette / solid black first. Fill your sketch as a solid black shape and squint at it (or shrink it way down). If you can still tell the pose and the weapon direction from the silhouette alone, the pose is doing its job. This is the single most common professional trick for readability and it costs nothing extra — you're already drawing in B&W.
  3. Exaggerate more than feels natural. Game sprites are usually viewed small and fast, mid-combat. A windup that looks "reasonable" at full size usually reads as nothing happening once it's 28-48px tall on screen. Push the anticipation pose further back, the contact pose further extended, than your instinct says to.
  4. Use the 12 principles of animation as a checklist, not a rulebook. The two that matter most for a first sprite: squash and stretch (a body that never deforms reads as stiff/robotic) and anticipation (a wind-up before the real motion — this repo's own bosses use a literal glowing-circle "telegraph" before every attack for exactly this reason, see the Common Visual Language notes near the bottom of this page).
  5. Animate on 2s, or even 3s, as a beginner. "On 2s" means one drawing covers 2 frames of screen time instead of 1 — half the drawings, and it's the industry-standard economy setting even for professional 2D games, not a beginner shortcut you'll need to "graduate" from. Most of this game's attacks are already scoped in short frame counts (see the per-attack "Telegraph (X frames)" / "Active (X frames)" numbers throughout this doc) — that maps directly onto a handful of hand-drawn key poses per attack, not a full frame-by-frame animation.
  6. Color and shading are a separate, later pass. A completely standard order: (1) rough thumbnail sketch → (2) clean silhouette/line pose → (3) flat color fill (no shading) → (4) optional simple shading (one highlight, one shadow tone). You can stop at step 3 and still have a usable game sprite — flat-color pixel/vector art is a legitimate, common style, not an unfinished one.

What actually matters for THIS game specifically

Telegraph readability

Every boss/enemy attack in this game has a distinct wind-up ("telegraph") before it becomes dangerous — a growing glow, an arrow, a charging circle. When sketching an attack pose, draw the telegraph pose and the active-hit pose as two clearly different silhouettes. That contrast is the actual gameplay-readable signal, more than color ever is.

Size discipline

Sprites here are small — the player is 24×32px, a normal enemy ~28×28px, even the final boss is only 48×56px (see the Size Reference table at the bottom of this page). Sketch at a size close to that early, or at least check your sketch shrunk down, so you're not designing detail that disappears at game scale.

Keyframes over full animation

This doc breaks every attack into named beats (Telegraph → Active → Recovery/Stagger). You don't need to draw every frame — draw one clean pose per beat first. That's already usable in anim_editor.html, which lets you drag-resize hitboxes and set per-frame durations around whatever poses you provide.

Grayscale value, not color, sells motion

Squash/stretch, motion lines, and value contrast (a lighter flash frame on hit-contact) read clearly in pure black-and-white. The existing code already does a lot of the "juice" with color overlays and particles (see Common Visual Language below) — your sketch's job is just a clear, readable pose underneath that.

If you want a next step beyond pencil-and-paper

Free/cheap tools built specifically for this kind of small-sprite, frame-based work (not general illustration software): Aseprite (paid, industry-standard for pixel/game sprites, has onion-skinning built in) or Piskel (free, browser-based, good enough for a first pass). Either works fine directly off a scanned/photographed pencil sketch — trace over it at low opacity rather than trying to draw pixel-perfect from scratch immediately.

Bottom line: sketching in black-and-white right now is not a workaround — it's step one of the normal process, and it's the step where the actual design work (does this pose read? does this attack look different from that attack?) happens. Coloring is a later, separable concern. Bring sketches for the poses/beats below and they're immediately useful even with zero color.

1. Origin Spine

(The Fracture, Echo Bridge, Crystal Cavern, Upper Ruins, The Vault, The Rift, The Antechamber, Hollow Core, Tutorial/Spawn)

Rooms (in graph order)

  • Spawn Area — 1 cosmetic upgrade. One-way to Tutorial Area.
  • Tutorial Area — one-way to The Fracture, part 1.
  • The Fracture, part 1 — fast travel node. Branches to part 2, part 3, and Crag Entrance (requires Phase Dash).
  • The Fracture, part 3 — branches to part 4, and (Void Tether required) to Chrono-Space Rift, Echo.
  • The Fracture, part 2 — branches to Mirror Veil, Gate.
  • The Fracture, part 4 — leads to Echo Bridge, part 1.
  • Echo Bridge, part 1 — meet the Child (mandatory, +1 max health). On first entry, mandatory detour to Echo Bridge, Prison. Branches to Crystal Cavern, Upper Ruins, Timeline X Roads Room 1, and (once the prison sequence ends) The Void Expanse Room 1 and (Void Tether path) Observatory Room 2.
  • Echo Bridge, Prison — 1 cosmetic upgrade. No way back to X Roads Room 1 — only forward to Timeline X Roads Room 2.
  • Crystal Cavern — unlocks Shard Shot (needed to leave). Entry requires the Child choice resolved. Branches to Timeline X Roads Room 1, Echoing Abyss Room 1 (requires Phase Dash), and (Void Tether path) Timeline X Roads Room 3.
  • Upper Ruins — branches to Pacifist Region.
  • The Vault, Room 1 — fast travel node. Branches to Polar Shift Room 1, The Vault Room 2, Graviton Core Room 1, The Rift.
  • The Vault, Room 2 — 1 Lore Pip. Leads to The Rift.
  • The Rift — entry requires Stillpoint + child_choice_resolved + Phase Dash. 1 lore pip. Branches to Polar Shift Room 1 and The Antechamber.
  • The Antechamber — branches to Hollow Core and (one-way, requires Phase Dash) back to Spawn Area, closing the loop into the Tutorial Area's Final Boss Fight room.
  • Hollow Core — 1 lore pip.

Ambience

Not visually specified per-room beyond the game's general dark violet/teal palette; no dedicated REGION_STYLES decoration exists for the spine.

Music

  • The Fracture (all 4 parts) shares chrono_fracture.ogg ("Transmission," Dark Sci-Fi Audio Pack, SRG774, CC0) with Chrono-Space Rift — described as looping/temporal, ties to the loop-reset ending.
  • The Vault shares vault_void_expanse.ogg ("The Depths of Hell," Joth, CC0) with Hollow Core, The Rift, and The Void Expanse — described as heavy, isolating, "closing in on the end."
  • Crystal Cavern has no separately-listed track; falls back to the default hub music unless mapped elsewhere.

Hazards

Not specified as region-unique — standard platform/enemy encounters.

Abilities / mechanics

Shard Shot is granted at Crystal Cavern (its own wall puzzle needs the ability in-room to solve). The Vault holds a Fracture Pip at its altar and is the spine's central hub, with four planned exits.

Lore flavor

The Fracture is a war-memorial mural/mystery whose meaning only lands at the ending — a single point of light on a silhouetted skyline, cracking outward, later recontextualized as the exact shape of the loop's reset. The Vault is where a hooded silhouette (implied to be the player's own past self) pocketed one Stillpoint shard from a table before armored figures seized the rest — "the one Stillpoint that was stolen back from her."

No ability granted here. Enemies: Gravity Scavengers, Gravity Wells.

Lore flavor

Not a command wing but a gravitic-material extraction mine feeding the war effort — containment failed late in the war, and it's still a self-collapsing singularity "extracting" debris and people who worked it. No malice, just an operation that outlived its chain of command. The Sovereign's search-probes sent in were pulled into the collapse; their beacon lights still fall, transmitting "searching," never "clear."

When the player commits to a move (charging heavy attack, holding Stillpoint, aiming Shard Shot, or attacking), she reads it:

  • Phase 2+: Text popup "READ." (blue). Responds with a real counter-attack matched to what was read (heavy → Dash-Chain, Stillpoint → Lunge, Shard → Phase Dash, attack → Melee Swing).

After 6 total hits, "— ADAPTING —" text in lavender. She biases future attack selection toward countering whatever the player uses most (melee vs. ranged). After 5 consecutive hits in Phase 2+: "ENOUGH" text in gold, immediately fires a Charged Heavy.

Every ~10 seconds (600 frames), he attempts to rewind all damage taken during that cycle.

The cycle:

  1. Timer counts down from 600 to 0
  2. At 90 frames remaining (~1.5s before rewind), the flash window starts:
  3. Pulsing cyan ring around him
  4. Eyes turn white
  5. Cyan particles burst

Common Visual Language

  • Telegraph = growing glow/circle (the player needs to see it winding up)
  • Active hitbox = the actual weapon art (sword slash, charge body, beam line)
  • Stagger = vulnerable pose (the player's punish window)
  • Flash = hit confirmation (white flash for 8 frames on damage taken)
  • Invulnerability = flickering opacity (sine-wave alpha)
  • Phase transition = dramatic pause (90 frames, text popup, invulnerable flash)
